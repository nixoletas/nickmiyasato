It all started back in 2017 when I decided to join the army. No tech business was
running in my mind whatsoever. I was actually interested in music production and
someday becoming a new Hans Zimmer or Ludwig Göransson. Big fan here 👋

## No easy path

![Mud](../../../assets/posts/mud.jpg)

I was lost in life, I didn't know what to do. The army came as a quick solution
to my doubts. "Let's see what this is all about, can I resist the training and
sleep deprivation? Will I endure the hell week?"

It taught me a lot. Some things I learned the hard way, some I just knew I had to
pick up the pace on, otherwise it would be jail time.

By the age of 19, as 1st Lieutenant, I was the leader of the communications
platoon. Those were some good times, I had to grow up fast. But I also missed my
family — I was far from home, in another state, and didn't know anyone.

I spent 5 years of temporary army service chasing music. It didn't really work
and it took me a while to give up. Music shouldn't have been a job, it should
have been a hobby, and I was chasing the wrong career.

## The playground

So after a while I decided to focus on programming and did some online courses.
By 2023 I moved to the IT Section, and suddenly I had an entire small
infrastructure as a playground. Seven Dell PowerEdge servers, two NAS units,
Cisco firewalls, and Proxmox running several virtual machines.

![Server Room](../../../assets/posts/server-room.jpg)

I learned a lot from my team and we played a lot with open source projects.
[Docker](https://www.docker.com/) and [Linuxserver](https://www.linuxserver.io/)
helped a lot deploying simple solutions like
[Uptime Kuma](https://uptimekuma.co/), [Gitea](https://about.gitea.com/) and
[Nextcloud](https://nextcloud.com/), and we worked on network security with
[Wireshark](https://www.wireshark.org/) and
[Security Onion](https://securityonionsolutions.com/).

Then we started building our own. A helpdesk ticket system integrated with
Telegram. An app to control and register entrances and exits of the battalion.
And finally we rebuilt the Joomla intranet using Angular, Nginx, Strapi and
Node.js.

That is the part that stuck. I built those systems and then I was the one who had
to keep them running, which is a feedback loop most engineers never get.

## What I do now

Full stack software engineer in São Paulo. Three-plus years of web applications,
mobile apps and all sorts of backends — *TypeScript, Python, Bash, C# and
Flutter* are some of the languages I've used. Applications I've built serve more
than 10,000 daily active users.

It splits two ways today. Full-time I'm on data: healthcare analytics pipelines
on BigQuery, Dataform and Python, and the dashboards clinical and business teams
make decisions on. Alongside that there are a couple of startups I'm working
with.

Portuguese is my first language, English my second. I studied Systems Analysis
and Development at Descomplica.
